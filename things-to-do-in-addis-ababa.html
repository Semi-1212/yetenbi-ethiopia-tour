Know the city

About Addis Ababa: the “New Flower” of the Highlands

Addis Ababa means “new flower” in Amharic. The city was founded in 1886 by Emperor Menelik II and his wife, Empress Taytu Betul, who is credited with choosing the site near the Filwoha hot springs after the court had first camped on the colder, windier slopes of Mount Entoto. In 1889 it became the capital of the whole Ethiopian Empire, and it has been the heart of the country ever since.

Today Addis Ababa is the home of the African Union and the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa, which is why it is often called the political capital of Africa. At roughly 2,355 metres above sea level it is also one of the highest capital cities in the world, so days are mild and nights are cool for most of the year.

The city has no single obvious centre. Museums, cathedrals, markets, parks and viewpoints are spread across different districts, and that is exactly why a local guide and a good driver make such a difference: you spend your time at the sights, not in traffic.

Must-see attractions

Top Things to Do in Addis Ababa

These are the places we send nearly every first-time visitor to, from the story of human origins to the story of modern Ethiopia.

History

1National Museum of Ethiopia & Lucy

Home to the cast of Lucy, the famous Australopithecus afarensis skeleton discovered in Ethiopia's Afar region in 1974 and dated to about 3.2 million years ago. Beyond the fossils you will find royal regalia, religious artefacts and a gallery of Ethiopian art spanning several centuries.

1.5–3 hours Best in the morning
Culture

2Ethnological Museum

Set in Emperor Haile Selassie's former palace on the Addis Ababa University campus, this is widely regarded as one of the best museums in East Africa. Exhibits walk you through the crafts, music, religious art and traditions of Ethiopia's many peoples.

1–2 hours On the AAU campus
Modern landmark

3Unity Park

Once closed to the public, the grounds of the Grand Palace opened as Unity Park in 2019. Walk through restored imperial buildings, themed gardens and a small zoo. Next door, Friendship (Sheger) Park offers riverside walks and a relaxed local atmosphere.

3–4 hours Open Tue–Sun, closed Mondays
Views

4Entoto Mountain & Entoto Maryam

The hills above the city are where Menelik II and Taytu first settled. Today you can enjoy eucalyptus forest, hiking trails and sweeping views over Addis Ababa. Entoto Maryam church is where Menelik II was crowned in 1889, and nearby you can see the former palace and museum.

2–3 hours Go early for clear views
Markets

5Merkato (Addis Mercato)

One of Africa's largest open-air markets: a maze of spices, textiles, baskets, coffee, second-hand goods and everything in between. It is loud, crowded and unforgettable, and best explored with a guide who knows the lanes, the prices and where to leave your valuables behind.

1.5–2 hours Great for souvenirs
Sacred sites

6Holy Trinity & St. George's Cathedrals

Holy Trinity Cathedral (Kidist Selassie) is the most important Ethiopian Orthodox cathedral in the capital and the resting place of Emperor Haile Selassie. St. George's Cathedral, with its distinctive octagonal design, sits in the Piazza area and holds a small museum. Dress modestly and ask your guide about etiquette.

1 hour each Modest dress required
Taste Ethiopia

Food & Coffee: The Best Things to Do in Addis Ababa After Sightseeing

Ethiopia is the birthplace of coffee and home to a cuisine built around sharing. Eating well is not an extra in Addis Ababa; it is one of the main reasons to come.

The Ethiopian coffee ceremony

Green beans are roasted in front of you, ground, and brewed in a clay jebena, then served in three rounds known as abol, tona and baraka. The scent of burning frankincense often fills the room. It is a ritual of hospitality and conversation, and you should expect to be invited to stay a while.

Beyond the ceremony, Addis Ababa has a lively cafe scene. Historic roasters such as Tomoca are loved by locals and visitors alike, and many cafes sell freshly roasted beans to take home.

What to order

Injera & wat
Sour, spongy injera made from teff, served with spicy stews (wat), lentils, greens and salads. Eaten with your right hand from a shared platter. Try doro wat (chicken stew) and shiro (chickpea stew).
Tibs & kitfo
Sizzling tibs of sautÃ©ed beef or lamb, and kitfo, minced beef seasoned with spiced butter and mitmita. Ask for it cooked to your preference.
Fasting food
On fasting days, many Orthodox Christians eat vegan. This means restaurants serve wonderful plant-based platters, ideal for vegetarians and vegans.
Street bites & juice
Fresh layered fruit juices, sambusa pastries, roasted corn and more. A guided food tour is the easiest way to taste widely and safely.

Go beyond the highlights

More Things to Do in Addis Ababa

Once you have covered the essentials, these experiences show you another side of the city.

  1. Red Terror Martyrs' Memorial Museum

    A powerful and sobering memorial to the victims of the Derg regime's Red Terror, opened in 2010. Guides share personal stories and the history behind them. Entry has been reported as free with donations welcome; check current details with us before you go.

  2. Meskel Square

    The city's main public square, famous as the stage of the Meskel festival each September. Locals also jog and gather here, and it is a handy landmark for orientation.

  3. Shiro Meda & craft shopping

    For hand-woven shawls, scarves and traditional clothing, visit the Shiro Meda market. Boutiques and craft shops around the city sell leather goods, jewellery, baskets and coffee sets.

  4. Art galleries & Zoma Museum

    Addis Ababa has a growing contemporary art scene, from small galleries to the Zoma Museum, known for its architecture inspired by Ethiopian vernacular building traditions.

  5. Piazza & Bole nightlife

    Evenings bring live music, traditional dance and cultural dinners with eskista shoulder dancing, plus modern bars and restaurants in Bole. Ask us about our nightlife experiences.

  6. Day trips from the capital

    Within reach by road are places such as Debre Libanos Monastery and the lakes around Bishoftu. See our day trips.

Plan your timing

Best Time to Visit Addis Ababa & Festivals Not to Miss

Thanks to its altitude, Addis Ababa is pleasantly mild year-round. The main question is rain, and whether you want to catch a festival.

Oct – FebJun – Sep

Dry season: October to February

The most comfortable window for walking tours, viewpoints and day trips, with clear skies and fresh mornings. This is also peak season, so book guides and hotels early, especially around Timkat.

Rainy season: June to September

The main rains, with July and August the wettest. Showers often fall in the afternoon, so plan museums and indoor sights then and save viewpoints for the morning. The city is green, quieter and often cheaper.

Questions answered

Addis Ababa Travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Addis Ababa?

Two full days covers the major sights comfortably: the National Museum, Ethnological Museum, Entoto, Unity Park, Merkato and a coffee ceremony. One day is enough for the highlights, and three days lets you add food experiences, galleries and a day trip.

What is the best time to visit Addis Ababa?

October to February is the dry season and the most comfortable time to visit, with clear skies and mild days. The main rains fall from June to September, with July and August the wettest months.

Is Addis Ababa safe for tourists?

Addis Ababa is generally welcoming to visitors. As in any big city, watch your belongings in crowded places such as Merkato, avoid displaying valuables, and use a trusted guide or licensed transport. A local guide makes busy areas much easier to enjoy.

What is Addis Ababa famous for?

Addis Ababa is famous as the diplomatic capital of Africa and the headquarters of the African Union, for the fossil Lucy in the National Museum, for Merkato, one of Africa's largest open-air markets, and for its coffee culture and Ethiopian cuisine.

Can I see Addis Ababa on a layover?

Yes. With airport or hotel pickup, you can see highlights such as Entoto, a museum and a coffee ceremony in about 3 to 8 hours between flights. We run private and small-group layover tours.

How high is Addis Ababa and will I feel the altitude?

Addis Ababa sits at roughly 2,355 metres (about 7,700 feet). Most visitors feel fine, but some notice mild breathlessness or tiredness on the first day. Drink plenty of water, go easy on day one and take your time on hills such as Entoto.
